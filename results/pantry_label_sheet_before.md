# Idli Batter (2kg batch) — version: original

## Method

Wash and soak the rice and the urad dal separately for 4 to 6 hours, with the
fenugreek seeds in the dal bowl. Grind the dal first with ice-cold water until
light, fluffy and tripled in volume — it should hold a soft peak. Grind the
rice to a fine rava-like texture, slightly grainy between the fingers. Combine
both with the rock salt and mix by hand for a full 2 minutes; the warmth of
your hand kick-starts the wild fermentation. Ferment 8 to 12 hours at 28–32°C
until doubled and domed. Do not stir the risen batter. Scoop gently into
greased idli moulds and steam 12 minutes; rest 2 minutes before unmoulding.

# Thayir — Homemade Curd (set overnight) — version: original

## Method

Bring the milk just to a boil, then simmer 5 minutes to concentrate it
slightly — this gives a thicker set. Cool to 40–43°C: the milk should feel
distinctly warm, not hot, on a clean fingertip held for ten seconds. Whisk
the starter curd until smooth, stir it through the milk, cover, and leave
completely undisturbed for 6 to 8 hours at around 30°C. In cold weather, set
the pot inside a switched-off oven with the light on, or wrap it in a thick
towel next to a hot-water bottle. The curd is set when it jiggles as one mass
and the surface releases a little clear whey. Refrigerate immediately once
set; over-fermenting at room temperature turns it sharply sour by evening.
Save 30g as the starter for tomorrow's batch.

# Appam (lace-edged fermented rice hoppers) — version: original

## Method

Soak the raw rice 4 hours, then grind with the cooked rice and half the
coconut milk to a completely smooth batter. Stir in the toddy (or the yeast
mixture) and the sugar, and ferment 8 hours at room temperature until bubbly
and risen. Just before cooking, stir in the remaining coconut milk and the 7g
fine sea salt; the batter should coat a spoon thinly. Heat an appachatti,
pour in a small ladleful, and immediately lift and swirl the pan in one full
circle so the batter climbs the walls — this makes the crisp lace edge with
the soft, spongy centre. Cover and cook 2 minutes; the appam is done when the
edges release and the centre springs back.

# Kadumanga Achar (Kerala fermented tender mango pickle) — version: original

## Method

Wipe the mangoes dry — no water must touch them at any stage. Layer them
with the crystal salt in a sterilised bharani (ceramic pickle jar), close,
and shake once daily. Over 10 to 14 days the salt draws out the mango's own
brine and the fruit ferments in it, turning olive-drab and pleasantly tangy.
Then heat the gingelly oil, crackle the mustard seeds, switch off the heat,
and stir in the chilli powder, fenugreek powder and asafoetida. Cool the
spiced oil completely and fold it through the mangoes and their brine.
Return everything to the bharani. The pickle is eatable in a week but
matures over a month; it keeps a year at room temperature if only dry
spoons ever enter the jar.

# Nankhatai (Indian shortbread biscuits) — version: original

## Method

Beat the ghee and powdered sugar for 5 minutes until pale and fluffy. Sift
in the maida, besan, semolina, cardamom and baking soda, and bring the dough
together with your fingertips — do not knead, or the biscuits turn hard.
Chill the dough 20 minutes. Roll into 24 balls of about 22g, flatten
slightly, and score a shallow cross on each. Bake on a lined tray at 180°C
for 16 to 18 minutes, until the bases are pale gold and the tops have
cracked. Cool on the tray for 10 minutes before moving them — hot nankhatai
crumble at a touch.
